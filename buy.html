First-time buyers

Your first-home checklist

The idea of owning a home is exciting and nerve-racking at the same time. It is likely the biggest purchase you will make, so work through these six in order.

Read the full buyer tips
  1. 01

    Find the right agent

    There are a lot of realtors out there. Talk to more than one and see who you connect with. Learn what an agent owes their client, and ask how they work before you commit to anyone.

  2. 02

    Consider your price range

    Know what you can comfortably afford, not just what a lender will approve. Use the mortgage calculator to see what a payment looks like at different prices.

  3. 03

    Do your research

    Get familiar with homes in your price range. Look on your own too, and send JK what you find. The best searches are a team effort.

  4. 04

    Have a good credit score

    Your credit score is a big part of getting a mortgage approved. The higher it is, the better your chances and the easier it is for a lender to say yes. Check yours early so there are no surprises.

  5. 05

    Consider the location

    Think about what your days look like: highway access, schools, hospitals, shopping, family. A great house in the wrong spot stops feeling great quickly. See the areas JK works in.

  6. 06

    Get a pre-approval

    A mortgage pre-approval tells you what you can afford. Give a lender the documents they ask for and they will guide you. It also helps when you make an offer.

Before you sign

Costs people forget

The price of the home is not the whole bill. Plan for these, and ask JK or your lender to put real numbers on each one for your situation.

  • •

    Closing costs

    Legal fees, title insurance and taxes that are due when ownership transfers. Your lawyer will give you the list.

  • •

    Land transfer tax

    A tax paid when you buy. Ask your lawyer to confirm the amount and whether any first-time buyer relief applies to you.

  • •

    Mortgage default insurance

    With a smaller down payment, lenders generally require this insurance. The premium is usually added to your mortgage.

  • •

    Home inspection

    A paid inspection can be worth it before you commit, so you know what you are buying.

  • •

    Moving and set-up

    Movers, utility hookups, address changes, and the little things you want to change on day one.

  • •

    Ongoing ownership costs

    Property tax, home insurance, utilities and repairs. A home does not stop costing money once it is paid for.

Buyer FAQ

Questions buyers ask

Do I need a pre-approval before I start looking?

You are not required to, but it is the first thing JK would suggest. It tells you what you can afford before you fall for a home you cannot buy, and it shows sellers you are serious.

What does it cost to work with JK as a buyer?

Ask before you sign anything. In Ontario you will normally sign a written agreement with your agent that explains how they are paid. JK will walk you through it in plain English, and you can ask questions until it makes sense.

Should I make an offer with no conditions?

Many sellers like offers with fewer conditions, so it can make your offer look stronger. But conditions such as financing and inspection are also your safety net. JK will talk through the trade-off for the specific home before you decide.

Can I buy before I sell my current home?

Sometimes. It depends on your financing and your timeline, and it needs a conversation with your lender early. Tell JK about it in the form above and he will help you map the order of events.

What happens after my offer is accepted?

Your lawyer takes over the title transfer, you finish any conditions, and you move toward the closing date. You can visit the home before closing to check that nothing has changed. If something has, tell JK and your lawyer right away.

How long will it take to find a home?

It depends on your budget, the areas, how flexible you are and what is for sale. JK would rather tell you that honestly than promise a date.
